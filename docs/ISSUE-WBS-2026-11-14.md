## 1. Resolved — the outage was radio sleep, not a reboot

**Measured root cause.** After the head was woken from the Android app, all four ports returned
(22/8090/8080/9090) and `uptime` settled the question:

```
uptime_min=334.2   at Sat Nov 14 02:13:14 UTC 2026   -> booted ~20:39 Nov 13
/tmp/polaris-astro-boot.log: "Fri Nov 13 20:39:05 UTC 2026 polaris-astro boot hook"
```

The boot log timestamp matches `/proc/uptime` exactly, and the hook marker is present in
`/app/network_telnetd.sh` (`grep -c "polaris-astro boot hook"` → 1). The device had been up
continuously since 20:39 — **it never rebooted during this session**, and the boot hook ran
fine. This was plain radio sleep: the firmware powers the Wi-Fi down 60 s after the last client
disconnects, and while the mount is unaligned `wifi-keepalive` deliberately will not hold a
connection open (connecting unaligned triggers the Benro compass-calibration prompt).

**Two earlier claims are withdrawn:**
1. *"Rebooted to stock"* — inferred from a `GoAhead-Webs` banner that was **never the Polaris**.
2. *"ENOSPC killed the boot hook"* — plausible-looking (`install_astro.sh:127-129` documents
   exactly that failure) but contradicted by uptime and the hook marker.

### 1.1 The `192.168.0.1` collision — why the outage was misdiagnosed
While the AP was down, `192.168.0.1` still answered on :80/443. It was **not** the Polaris:
the browser rendered **"CGNV4-FX4 Login — Hitron Technologies"**, a router admin page. My host
sits on `192.168.68.0/24` (gateway `.68.1`), so when the AP disappeared, traffic to
`192.168.0.1` was routed to the ISP modem instead.

**Consequence for future diagnosis:** a live :80 on `192.168.0.1` is not evidence the head is
up. Confirm with `/api/link` returning our JSON, or check the page title, before concluding
anything about patch state. A bare TCP connect or HTTP 200 can be answered by a different device.
